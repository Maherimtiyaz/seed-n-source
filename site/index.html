Our founding story

Every Company You Admire Was Once a Seed in the Dark

This week, and every week, the paper digs up one YC company, from its first idea down to its code.

By the Seed & Source deskRead time: 2 minutes

By the time a company makes the front page, its beginning is long out of sight. The billion-dollar round, the launch on every timeline, the product your friends suddenly use: it all looks like it happened overnight. It never does.

Every one of those companies started as a seed. A founder annoyed by a problem that wouldn't go away. A first version nobody asked for. An investor who said no. Then, quietly, roots: a database someone chose, an open-source library that saved months, a decision that would matter for years.

Most startup writing covers only what grew above ground. Funding news without the code. Engineering posts without the business. Founder legends without the numbers.

Seed & Source reports both halves. Each Tuesday this paper takes one company and follows it from seed to bloom. Every claim is linked to the place we found it.

Day 0

A1 · The Seed

It Starts Where Nobody Is Looking

An annoyed founder, a rough sketch, and usually a “no.”

A founder gets fed up with a problem that won't go away. They build a rough answer, pitch it, and are often turned down. No logo yet, no users, no headline.

By the time you read about a company, this part has been buried for years. We dig it up: who was annoyed, by what, and the first version they shipped.

In every issue: The Story where the idea came from

A2 · The Roots

Plants Grow Roots Before Anyone Sees a Leaf

Startups do too. Before the first customer, there is a stack.

A database someone picked. An open-source library that saved six months. An engineering decision that holds the whole thing up for years.

More of it is public than ever, in open repos, changelogs and engineering blogs. We read them and explain the one decision that matters most, in plain words.

In every issue: Under the Hood the stack and the decision behind it

B1 · The Sprout

Then, One Morning, Something Breaks the Surface

The first customers, and the moment founders remember.

A launch finally lands. Sometimes it's a new feature; surprisingly often it's a new sentence that explains the product better than the old one did.

That turning point is the easiest part to learn from. We find it, and we show you the proof.

In every issue: The Story the moment it clicked

B2 · The Stem

A Plant Carries Water Up. A Company Carries Money In.

Pricing, funding and the numbers founders rarely share.

How does it charge? Who invested, and when? How big did the team get? The part founders talk about least is the part readers want most.

We print only figures the company or reputable press reported, and we label outside estimates as estimates.

In every issue: The Business Side how it makes money

C1 · The Bloom

The Part Everyone Sees

It looks overnight. It never is.

The big round, the launch on every timeline, the company your friends suddenly use. This is where most coverage starts. In this paper, it's where the story ends.
