## 🌱 The Story

In 2014, Paul Copplestone pitched an investor on an idea about making databases easier for developers. It fell flat. He tried again about six years later. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/04/database-startup-supabase-raises-500-million-10point5-billion-valuation.html))

The second attempt started with a problem, not a pitch. At his company Nimbus, Paul was building a chat-style app. Most of the data lived in Postgres, but the real-time features ran on Firebase, and Firebase hit its scaling limits. So he rebuilt the real-time layer on top of Postgres itself: *"I realized I could build this real-time feature on top of Postgres itself."* He open-sourced it, developers got interested, and that interest became a company. He brought in Ant Wilson, whom he knew from the Entrepreneur First program, as co-founder and CTO, and they applied to Y Combinator. Supabase joined the Summer 2020 batch. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/), [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/supabase))

**The moment it clicked was a change of words, not features.** Paul changed the tagline to "Open-source Firebase alternative". The next day someone posted it to Hacker News and it reached the top. His takeaway: product-market fit can come down to positioning. The product barely changed; what changed was that developers instantly understood what it was for. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

The Hacker News comments became the roadmap. People asked for authentication, because Firebase had it. Supabase built it, storing users inside the Postgres database itself, and shipped it before YC Demo Day. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

Then came the habit they're known for: **Launch Weeks**. Instead of one launch, a week of launches, every few months. Paul says each one produced roughly "20% bumps" in usage. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

**Then the AI wave arrived, and Supabase happened to be standing where it landed.** Paul describes three phases:
1. **Embeddings:** Supabase was among the first to offer `pgvector`, so developers could store AI embeddings next to their normal data.
2. **AI app builders (end of 2024):** "End of 2024 was when the likes of Bolt and Lovable launched." Their users created databases at enormous volume, so Supabase built *Supabase for Platforms* in 2025 for companies that create databases on their users' behalf.
3. **Coding agents (2026):** Claude Code and Codex now drive a large share of new databases through the command line and MCP.

([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

By June 2026, Supabase said **database launches had grown 600% in a year**, **more than 60% of new databases were launched by some kind of AI tool**, and **nearly 10 million developers** build on it. ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/blog/series-f))

## 💰 The Business Side

**Funding (as of June 2026):**

| When | Round | Valuation |
|---|---|---|
| June 2026 | $500M Series F, led by GIC | $10B pre-money / $10.5B after the round |
| October 2025 | Previous round | about half the June valuation ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/04/database-startup-supabase-raises-500-million-10point5-billion-valuation.html)) |

All existing investors joined the Series F, Stripe invested a second time, and Georgian and Salesforce Ventures came in as new investors. ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/blog/series-f)) CNBC reports **more than 250,000 customers** and **about 350 employees**. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/04/database-startup-supabase-raises-500-million-10point5-billion-valuation.html))

**How an open-source company makes money:**
- Almost all the code is under permissive licenses ("MIT, Apache 2 or Postgres"). Only platform code such as billing is closed.
- Self-hosting is free, and Paul says they put no tracking on it.
- The money comes from the **hosted platform**: developers who'd rather pay than run it themselves. Paul says open source is not a funnel to gated features.

([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

**A sales experiment worth stealing.** Supabase is product-led, with sales added on top. To prove sales actually helped, they compared customers who talked to a success rep with those who didn't: about **125% quarter-over-quarter growth vs 25%**. Reps are paid only on the **uplift over that control group**, not on all revenue they touch. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

**Revenue:** not disclosed. One outside estimate (Sacra) puts annualized revenue around $251M at the end of October 2026; treat that as an estimate, not a reported number. ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/supabase/))

**Team:** fully remote and asynchronous, no offices. For years they hired only for "hair-on-fire" problems; Paul admits they kept that rule too long, and in mid-2025 switched to hiring as fast as they could. ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-supabase-became-the-essential-infrastructure-for-the-ai-era-paul-copplestone-co-founder-ceo/))

## 🔧 Under the Hood

Supabase isn't one giant program. It's a set of open-source tools wrapped around a normal Postgres database, each doing one job:

| Piece | Language | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Postgres** | C | The database itself. Your app's data lives here |
| **PostgREST** | Haskell | Reads your tables and automatically gives you a REST API for them |
| **GoTrue** | Go | Sign-up, login and tokens |
| **Realtime** | Elixir | Pushes database changes to browsers over WebSockets |
| **Supavisor** | Elixir | Shares database connections between many clients |
| **Kong** | — | The front door that routes requests to the right service |

([Supabase architecture docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/getting-started/architecture))

**The clever decision: security lives in the database.** GoTrue issues a token when you log in. Postgres reads that token and applies **Row Level Security** rules, like "a user can only read rows where `user_id` is their own id". Because the rules sit in the database rather than in app code, the API PostgREST generates is safe to call straight from a browser. That's why an AI app builder can create a working backend in seconds: tables plus rules equals an API. ([Supabase docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/getting-started/intro))

**What's next:** alongside the Series F, Supabase previewed **Multigres**, an open-source (Apache 2.0) layer for scaling Postgres to very large workloads. ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/blog/series-f))

## 🧠 What builders can take from it

1. **Positioning can be the product.** "Real-time Postgres" didn't land. "Open-source Firebase alternative" reached the top of Hacker News the next day. Describe what you are in words your buyer already uses.
2. **Let your first public thread write the roadmap.** Auth came straight from Hacker News comments.
3. **Make launches a ritual.** Launch Weeks turned shipping into repeatable marketing.
4. **Bet on a standard, not a proprietary format.** Building on Postgres meant every Postgres improvement, and every developer's existing knowledge, worked in their favor.
5. **Measure sales against a control group.** If you can't show the uplift, you're paying for revenue that would have come anyway.

## 📡 AI Radar

**Noeri: AI that proposes its own improvements, and asks first.** Yejin Choi and Carlos Guestrin launched Noeri on November 16 with $110 million, led by Menlo Ventures, with Madrona, Kindred, Wing, NEA, Snowflake and Cisco also in. Noeri's systems study a company's data and workflows, then propose changes: a better instruction, a different tool, a different model for a step. Every change comes with an explanation and waits for a person to approve it. The founders have shipped before: Guestrin helped build XGBoost and co-founded Turi, which Apple bought in 2016. ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/16/noeri-raises-110m-to-help-companies-build-living-ai-systems-that-self-improve-over-time/))
*Why builders should care:* "self-improving AI" usually sounds scary to a buyer. Putting a human approval step in the product itself is how Noeri makes it sellable.

**Genesis AI: robots that learn from a glove.** Genesis AI raised a $500 million Series A on November 17, co-led by Khosla Ventures, Premji Invest and Eclipse ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raises-500-million-to-deploy-electric-glove-wearing-robots/)), at a $3.5 billion valuation with Nvidia also investing ([Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-17/nvidia-backed-genesis-ai-raises-funds-at-3-5-billion-valuation)). Its trick is the training data. More than 100 workers wear a sensor glove and headset cameras to record how human hands actually grip things, and the company's Eno robots wear the same glove, with hands built to human proportions. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/genesis-ai-raises-500-million-to-deploy-electric-glove-wearing-robots/))
*Why builders should care:* in robotics, data is the moat. Genesis built its own way to collect it instead of waiting for someone else's.
